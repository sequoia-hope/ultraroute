## 1. Crates that move

### kicad-pcb (6,909 lines; 53 unit tests, 37 in `tests/`)

What it is: the project's own reader and writer for KiCad boards, `.kicad_pro` rules and
`.kicad_dru`, with no pcbnew. Modules: `sexpr`, `read`, `copper`, `outline`, `model`, `write`,
`rules`, `dru`, `place`, `canon`, `text`.

Solid:
- KiCad 6 to 10 boards parse; the corpus (about 1,180 usable PCBench boards) loads and is
  judged against `kicad-cli` daily. Golden tests (`tests/golden.rs`), real boards
  (`tests/real_boards.rs`), placement (`tests/place.rs`).
- The writer is a byte splice: it cuts removed spans and appends new copper, so everything it
  does not understand is preserved as written. That property is worth keeping.
- `dru.rs` and `board/custom.rs` are a real rule engine for `.kicad_dru`, with KiCad's
  semantics probed case by case (memory `kicad-coverage`).
- `canon.rs`: a canonical item order, so file order does not change results.
- `tests/gpl_boundary.rs` checks that no GPL code is linked.

Known wrong or thin (from `docs/review/REVIEW-2026-10-28.md` 1.8; remediation Stage 9 then fixed
many; today I only confirmed by search that code now exists for net ties, curve edges, oval
holes, an arc writer and `locked`, not that each is right):
- KiCad 5 boards do not parse; the benchmark converts them with pcbnew first, and rewrites
  duplicate footprint uuids.
- Blind and buried vias appear only in `rules.rs`; I found no reading or writing of them.
- Per-pad `padstack`, per-via tenting, several net classes on one net, teardrops, text boxes
  and dimensions on copper: recheck each against 1.8's table.
- Arc tracks are held as chords (5 um chord error) on the inside of the arc; recheck that
  clearance against a chord cannot pass copper too close to the true arc.
- The file is parsed more than once per run.

No test covers: schematics (none are read), netlists (Composer's reader), writing groups.

What I would do differently in the move: make `kicad` the only writer too. Today blocks,
placements and pours are applied by Python through pcbnew in Composer and in
`tools/add_block.py`, `tools/refill_zones.py`.

### board (5,153 lines; 17 unit tests, 78 in `tests/`)

Comes: `geom.rs` (integer nanometres), `model.rs` (Board, Item, Kind, ItemId, Block,
loading), `check.rs`, `custom.rs`, `fill.rs`, the plain connectivity in `connect.rs`, and
`ZoneArea` out of `regions.rs`. Stays behind: `work.rs`, `probe.rs`, `spokes.rs`, the rest of
`regions.rs`, and `firmly_joined`, `joint_neck`, `end_neck`, `meets_squarely` in `connect.rs`.
Cuts needed (checked today): `model.rs` has fields typed from `regions.rs` at lines 350, 352,
361; `check.rs` calls `work` five times and `regions` once; `connect.rs` calls `work` at
237-238.

Solid:
- `check.rs` agrees with KiCad 9.0.8 on the fixtures (`tests/kicad_agreement.rs`: expected
  numbers are what `kicad-cli pcb drc` reported) and, in practice, across the corpus: our
  open count matches KiCad's unconnected count, which peers confirmed independently.
- `tests/custom_rules.rs`, `mask.rs`, `thermals.rs`, `blocks.rs`, `kicad5.rs`, `index.rs`.

Known wrong or thin:
- `check` covers clearance, edge, hole, hole-to-hole, keepout. Necks and connection width,
  most solder-mask checks, annular ring and courtyards are judged only by `kicad-cli`.
- `fill.rs` is our zone fill, not KiCad's. The benchmark refills with KiCad before DRC
  (`tools/refill_zones.py`) for that reason. Treat our fill as a preview.
- The spatial index is a hash map with a sort per query, and several callers used
  `Board::clone` as undo. Fine for a foundation crate; do not copy the habit.
- `Limits::of` was recomputed on every `check_item` (REVIEW #64); recheck.
- The model has items, not wires, and no plan. Both are new (`from-zero.md`).
- Slots as circles, net ties: see kicad-pcb above; recheck.

### kicad-text (1,776 lines, GPL-3.0)

Text outlines from KiCad's own font data. A separate binary, run as a process. A router or
checker without it beside the binary treats text as boxes, which buries pads on some boards;
the benchmark refuses a binary that lacks it unless told otherwise.

## 2. Benchmark and review tools (`tools/` to `bench/`)

- `pcbench.py`: prepare, run, report, compare, rejudge, tiers, ab. Judging is `kicad-cli`
  DRC after a KiCad refill, against the board's own rules, plus necks, acid traps
  (`dfm.py`) and tidiness (`tidy.py`) apart from the verdict. `ab` runs two binaries
  interleaved in one pool; anything else biases the slower side. Tiers: smoke, focus, gate
  (315 boards), full (1,182). CPU-clock timeouts.
- `scoreboard.py`, `lookset.py` + `lookset.json`, `pcbench_schematics.py`: written today.
- **Two faults in the measure, found today, not yet fixed:**
  1. PCBench's `processed.kicad_pcb` has the designers' pours removed. 283 of the gate
     tier's 314 boards had pours, 40 of the 57 multi-layer boards on an inner layer. Every
     designer comparison so far is on boards without the designers' planes.
  2. `tidy.py` measures angles and layer direction in the board's x and y. A board routed in
     a turned frame (motionhexa) scores as 89% off-angle.
- Paths: the tools find the corpus at `ROOT/PCBench` (gitignored, not redistributable) and
  the lab at `ROOT.parent/ultraroute-lab`. The baseline binary is a pinned directory,
  `ultraroute-lab/bin/sqpad-9512c88/` (router and `kicad-text` together).
- Runs go through `~/.claude/bin/reserve` and under `systemd-run` with a memory cap.

## 7. Rules worked by that are written nowhere else

1. Look at the picture, designer beside ours, before quoting a number. Tables missed for
   weeks what one look showed.
2. A score is only as good as its input: check what the benchmark board actually contains
   (pours, text, rules) before trusting a comparison.
3. Compare two binaries only interleaved in one pool, same seed, same clock.
4. A run that is "untouched by any deadline" is still not exact: sub-deadlines existed. New
   work is bounded by counts, never by time.
5. KiCad is the judge. Our checker and fill exist to be fast and to agree with it; where they
   disagree, KiCad is right until proven otherwise on a probe board.
6. Report what was hand-fed, what was measured and what was inferred, separately.
7. Agent-written reports are audited against the diff; several described code that was not
   there.
8. Nothing bespoke per board. A fix that wins one board is suspect until the gate tier
   agrees.
9. Benchmark boards and fetched schematics are other people's work, often unlicensed: they
   stay out of every repository and every published page.
