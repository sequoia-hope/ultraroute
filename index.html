ultraroute

Building

A fast, GPU-capable, host-friendly KiCad autorouter, built on a fork of CopperRoute, and a bench for comparing routing approaches.

Where it stands

CopperRoute is cloned into copperroute/ on a local governance branch. It builds, and its benchmark harness runs against KiCad-refereed PCBench boards. The router now governs itself by default: it runs niced, uses at most half the cores, and stops gracefully at a memory ceiling while still writing the session. Routing goes through a pluggable RouterBackend trait. maze is the existing router; none is the scoring floor. Backends now include grid routing with negotiated congestion (grid) and part nudging (nudge). The aim of nudging is to automate what a designer does by hand: move the parts that stop a connection from routing. Parts the designer wants left alone are locked with --lock. The fork is pushed to sequoia-hope/copperroute@governance.

Route job: bluebuzz

Input is the tamed DSN route.py feeds Freerouting (bluebuzz 6c5bd5e): 6 layers with only 3 routable, 696 keepouts, and 38 connections open before routing. Every attempt is scored by the same copperroute drc against the bare input. "Unconnected" counts nets with an open connection: the DRC lists one entry per such net, not one per missing connection. Leaderboard (24 attempts), best recipe.

  • Rescored with the KiCad project rules (2026-10-23): the 6-unconnected best below has 11 hole-to-hole violations, and the 10-unconnected grid route has 10. The best result with no new violations is now 12 unconnected (grid + nudge with the via fix and project rules; 24 vias, 206 mm, 482 s), ahead of the earlier clean best of 14. No part moved: the nudge found nothing to improve, so this is the grid route under the project rules. It is now best/: the leaderboard ranks clean results (no new violations) first
  • Before rescoring: 6 unconnected, 0 new violations, by moving C95 0.08 mm (+0.072, +0.025). J1, U7 and U20–U23 locked. Recipe: --backend nudge --set router.fanout.enabled=false --lock J1,U7,U20,U21,U22,U23 with COPPERROUTE_NUDGE_BASE=grid COPPERROUTE_GRID_NEGOTIATE=1 COPPERROUTE_GRID_CELL_MM=0.05 (282 s; 39 vias, 271 mm). Blame named C95 as the part in TAC_SDO's way. Four connections closed after the move, partly because the router took a different path once the board changed
  • What copperroute blame says about the 6 still open: VBAT and 3V3_SYS have one end buried in the tamed DSN's keepouts and planes (no free grid cell at all); 3V3_SENS, MOTOR2_PWM, TAC_SDO and USB_DM_C cross only other nets' routed copper, so they are congestion, not placement
  • Best without moving parts: 10 unconnected, 0 new violations. Recipe: --backend grid --set router.fanout.enabled=false with COPPERROUTE_GRID_NEGOTIATE=1 COPPERROUTE_GRID_CELL_MM=0.05 (146 s; PathFinder on 12 CPU threads, then maze). Costs copper: 37 vias, 265 mm. Without negotiation: 13
  • Nudge (J4 −0.5 mm, approved): 17 alone. On top of grid it adds nothing (13 → 13)
  • Best without moving parts: 18 unconnected, 0 new violations, 9 vias, 163.9 mm. Recipe: --set router.fanout.enabled=false, COPPERROUTE_OBSTACLE_HEURISTIC=1 and COPPERROUTE_REGION_HISTORY=0.25. Shortest copper: 153.7 mm with 10 vias (heuristic only)
  • Freerouting 2.4.1 governed: also 18, but 173 mm, 249 s, 1.3 GB. Freerouting master stalls past 600 s
  • Ripup history (factors 0.25–4): no effect. Bluebuzz's failures aren't nets fighting over space
  • Obstacle-aware heuristic: also 18. It's sharper in only about 1% of searches, but there by up to 11.6×
  • Fixed copper and keepouts never fully wall off a start on the routable layers, so the remaining 18 aren't impossible by geometry alone. Obstacle grid render. Retracted: an earlier "11 of 18 nets walled in" reading came from starts on the unroutable plane layers

Working

  • CopperRoute and Freerouting build locally
  • Benchmark harness with KiCad DRC referee on 42 PCBench boards
  • copper-governor: nice 10, RSS watchdog with soft stop and hard cancel, --nice and --max-memory
  • Default thread cap cut from cores−1 to cores/2
  • RouterBackend trait and --backend flag
  • PCB skill: governed-autorouter section in references/routing.md
  • wgpu 30 sees the RTX 3060 (Vulkan; int64 atomics, subgroups, timestamps)
  • Fork pushed: sequoia-hope/copperroute@governance; default job timeout now 30 min
  • tools/route_job.py: governed portfolio routing per board, keeps the best session and its recipe
  • Opt-in per-net ripup history (PathFinder history term), COPPERROUTE_RIPUP_HISTORY
  • No stacked vias: the grid stamps every drilled hole and keeps vias the hole-to-hole distance apart for all nets, own net included; an existing via's centre stays open to its net for reuse. Commit reuses vias at the same point and refuses a path whose via would still clash. Earring: 42 stacked holes, 12 hole-to-hole and 4 shorts down to 0 in KiCad. The runner now hands the board's .kicad_pro to router and referee, and --rescore rescored every kept session
  • Three bugs found by checking against KiCad: (1) moving a part could move its pins twice, depending on whether each pin's centre was cached, so a reloaded nudged session read every net on the moved part as open (earring scored 31 open instead of 2). (2) --lock wrote lock_type position into the session, which KiCad refuses to import. (3) Nudge counted lone dangling items as open connections. All fixed and tested. The runner now matches violations by what they are between, not by position, so a moved part keeps its old violations
  • Targeted nudge and copperroute blame: for each open connection, route it on its net's grid with movable parts passable at a price that grows with how deep the path intrudes into their clearance. Routed copper is a wall (or priced). The intrusions on the cheapest path become per-part displacements, solved by cyclic projection; a part pushed from both sides is reported as unmovable. Nudge tries these moves smallest first, instead of 16 blind guesses per part. --lock REF,... keeps parts in place. blame also tells apart connections that are walled off, pinned by their own part's pins, or blocked by congestion
  • Milestone 5, negotiated congestion (PathFinder): per-net zones, rising present cost and history, greedy legal commit; connections from a spanning tree per net (the DRC lists one entry per open net). Pad escape stubs checked by exact geometry. Runs on governed CPU threads, not the GPU: on bluebuzz grids (1.79M nodes), 32 cost fields take 0.05 s on 12 CPU threads against about 3 s batched on the RTX 3060, and GAMER-style sweeps were slower still. All GPU kernels match the CPU exactly and stay as tested experiments. Parallel negotiation on bm05: 48.8 s → 5.1 s, no new violations
  • GPU router, Milestone 4: copper-gpu builds a conservative per-net grid (any accepted path is legal), computes integer cost fields with relax.wgsl on wgpu, identical node for node to the CPU Dijkstra reference, and commits paths ending exactly on pin and via centres. --backend grid routes on the grid first, then maze finishes. No new DRC violations on bm05 or bluebuzz. Not faster than CPU yet on small grids (0.13 vs 0.08 s for six fields)
  • settle port done (see docs/morph-port-plan.md): pressure, group stepping, copper shove/drag, warp field (CG, rubber's claim rounds), blocker rip-and-reroute, ghost placement and site search, length caps, and the driver. copperroute settle BOARD --ses ROUTED.ses --targets t.json -o OUT.ses. Every piece is tested against the independent DRC on routed DAC2020 bm05. Warping accepts 23 of 30 moves, shoving 24; no lost connections, no added violations
  • Oracle fixed and made referee-consistent. (1) Pins now really move: the component moves before its items re-enter the search tree. (2) Clearance is judged like the DRC and KiCad, using each pad's net-class clearance, which on these boards is far larger than the pin's item class (0.152 vs 0.038 mm on DAC2020). Measured as real gaps. (3) Grandfathered pairs may not lose distance. (4) push(), settle's pressure: blocked parts shove free neighbours aside
  • --backend nudge: route, then try line-searched moves of parts with open connections on board clones, rerouting only what's open. A move is kept only if it closes more. 506 s on bluebuzz, so it needs tuning
  • Session reader applies placement (translation only), so nudged sessions are scored where the parts really are. Round-trip tested; unmoved sessions score identically
  • copper-morph move oracle, ported from settle (lean-jiggle): exact-shape clearance conflicts with grandfathering, guard band, bisection line search. Attached routable copper is ripped for the router; fixed copper pins the part. Independent DRC confirms moves on tomu-fpga add no violations
  • Opt-in obstacle-aware admissible heuristic (survey E1), COPPERROUTE_OBSTACLE_HEURISTIC: per-run coarse obstacle grid plus a backward Dijkstra per search, h = max(box, coarse), byte-identical when off
