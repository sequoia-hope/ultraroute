Kissing joints: where they come from

Track ends that touch their net's copper without meeting it squarely: not on another track's end or centre line, nor in a pad or at a via's centre. Recorded by ULTRAROUTE_JOINT_TRACE after every stage and at marks inside the shove and the pruning. Branch fix/square-joints.

The shape of the problem

What makes them

Every gap is a whole grid step: 50 µm, 70.7 µm (a diagonal), 100 µm or 111.8 µm. A short link of the grid path was taken out after the path was laid, and the tracks either side were left joined only where their round ends overlap. Inside the shove: the region plough tidies up after every try (settle), and that runs the router's dangling-copper pruning (prune_dangling_of). The pruning removed a link as "dangling" or "covered" whenever the tracks on either side counted as firmly joined without it. It judged firmness by overlap width, and it counted an end lying inside the other track's round end as full width. Tracks one grid step apart passed that test, so the link that actually joined them went. Steps, before the fix (4 boards with the finest marks):

The fix

Firm now means square, the way drawn copper joins: a track end on another track's end or centre line, inside its straight body (which its own body then crosses at full width), or wholly inside its round end; at a via's centre or wholly inside the via; or inside a pad. There is no width threshold. A link is pruned only if its neighbours meet squarely without it.
